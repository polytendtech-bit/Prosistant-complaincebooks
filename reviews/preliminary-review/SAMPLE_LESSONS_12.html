1. A doorway handoff

Original FAM-001 · new provisional example

Situation

Maya brings Leo to the next room. Jordan is already supervising that room. Maya thinks Jordan has accepted Leo, but Jordan has not replied.

Practice

What should Maya do before leaving Leo with Jordan?

  1. Leave because Jordan can see the doorway.
  2. Ask Jordan to accept care of Leo, wait for a clear reply, and check Leo against the attendance list.
  3. Ask another child to watch Leo.
  4. Finish the paperwork in the office first.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: B

An adult must clearly accept responsibility. Seeing another adult nearby does not prove the handoff is complete.

Everyday example: It is like handing someone a full cup: make sure they have hold of it before you let go.

Demonstrate: Practice a handoff with a partner. Name the child, receive a reply, and check the attendance list.

Safety feedback: Leaving a child without an adult who has accepted care.

Maya把Leo带到隔壁教室。离开前，她应请Jordan明确接手，等对方回应，再按当日名单核对Leo。附近有成人，不代表已经完成照护交接。

Sources still to verify: HEADSTART_SUPERVISION

2. A toy in an infant's sleep space

Original FAM-017 · new provisional example

Situation

During a routine check, you see a stuffed toy in an infant's sleep space. The infant is breathing normally and has no signs of distress. A parent says the toy helps at home.

Practice

Which is the safest general teaching response?

  1. Keep the toy because the parent requested it.
  2. Cover the toy with a blanket.
  3. Remove the toy, maintain supervision, and explain the safe-sleep procedure to the parent.
  4. Wait for the next staff meeting before acting.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: C

A parent's familiar routine does not establish that an item is safe in an infant's sleep space. The final lesson needs current safe-sleep guidance and the applicable state rule.

Everyday example: A toy can be suitable for awake, supervised play without belonging in the sleep space.

Demonstrate: Use an empty demonstration sleep space to identify items that need checking against current guidance. Do not use a child for the demonstration.

Safety feedback: Keeping a known potential sleep hazard only because of parent preference.

例子中的婴儿没有紧急不适表现。先移走睡眠区域内的毛绒玩具，继续照看婴儿，再向家长解释安全睡眠做法。家中习惯不能代替安全要求。最终教材须核实现行睡眠指导与州规则。

Sources still to verify: NIH_SLEEP, CDC_SLEEP, CPSC_CRIB

3. Was the medicine already given?

Original FAM-023 · new provisional example

Situation

A child's medicine record is blank. One worker thinks a scheduled dose may already have been given. The child has no urgent symptoms in this example.

Practice

What should the next worker do?

  1. Give another dose so the record will be complete.
  2. Guess from the amount left in the bottle.
  3. Ask a child whether the medicine was given.
  4. Do not repeat the dose on a guess; keep the child supervised and use the medication plan's qualified clinical advice route.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: D

A blank entry does not prove no medicine was given. The person reviewing the record must not invent a dosing decision.

Everyday example: An unchecked delivery box does not prove a package never arrived; first find reliable information. Medicine decisions also need qualified clinical advice.

Demonstrate: Using a fictional record, identify what is known, what is uncertain, whom the plan tells you to contact, and how to record the actual advice and action.

Safety feedback: Giving a possible duplicate dose without appropriate direction.

空白给药记录不等于没有给过药。不要猜测后再给一次。继续照看儿童，按用药计划联系具备资质的医疗指导人员，并如实记录。若出现紧急症状，应立即采用相应急救流程。

Sources still to verify: CCDF, HEADSTART

4. A child tells you something worrying

Original FAM-049 · new provisional example

Situation

During play, a child says, “Someone hurt me last night.” No immediate medical emergency is apparent. You are the caregiver receiving the statement.

Practice

Which response best supports safety and a proper reporting process?

  1. Listen calmly, avoid leading questions, record the child's own words, and follow the applicable reporting duty without waiting to prove the allegation.
  2. Promise you will keep it secret.
  3. Interview the other children until the story is proven.
  4. Wait for the owner's permission before deciding whether a legally required report may be made.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: A

The caregiver's role is to support the child and follow reporting duties, not investigate the allegation. The jurisdiction-specific reporting trigger, recipient, and timing still need primary-source verification.

Everyday example: Write “Someone hurt me last night,” rather than changing it to your own conclusion about who did what.

Demonstrate: Locate the applicable official reporting instructions. Identify the reporting trigger, recipient, deadline, and whether any additional written follow-up is required.

Safety feedback: Delaying an already-required report to obtain proof or internal approval.

平静倾听，不承诺保密，不用诱导问题追问，也不自行调查。记录儿童原话，依法履行报告义务；不为收集证据或等待老板许可而拖延已经触发的报告。报告门槛、对象和时限须按所在州核实。

Sources still to verify: NY_REPORT, CCDF

5. The unlabeled snack

Original FAM-025 · new provisional example

Situation

A child has a documented food-allergy plan. A visitor brings cookies with no ingredient information. A suitable snack that has already been checked against the plan is available.

Practice

What should you offer the child?

  1. The cookie after removing visible nuts.
  2. The checked alternative, while keeping the uncertain cookies away from the child's food.
  3. A small bite of the cookie as a test.
  4. The cookie if another child ate one safely.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: B

The ingredients and cross-contact risk are unknown. Other children's reactions and removing visible pieces do not establish that this food fits the child's plan.

Everyday example: A label missing from a container means you cannot tell what is inside by looking at the lid.

Demonstrate: Practice checking a fictional snack label against a fictional allergy plan. For emergency symptoms, use the child's emergency plan rather than this routine snack exercise.

Safety feedback: Using a taste test to determine whether a child's allergen is present.

按该儿童的过敏计划提供已经核实的替代点心，不试吃，不靠挑掉可见坚果来判断安全。其他儿童吃后没事，也不能证明这个儿童可以吃。

Sources still to verify: CDC_ALLERGY

6. Can one more child attend?

Original FAM-005 · new provisional example

Situation

A family requests an extra day. You have not yet checked the children's ages, attendance, qualified staff, or the current license conditions.

Practice

Can you determine from these facts that the extra attendance is permitted?

  1. Yes, because only one child is being added.
  2. Yes, because a parent agrees to the arrangement.
  3. No. Obtain the relevant facts and apply the current rules and license conditions before confirming attendance.
  4. No program may ever accept an extra day.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: C

Neither permission nor a universal refusal follows from the facts given. A legal calculation needs the applicable rule and the actual age and staffing information.

Everyday example: A room with an empty chair may still lack a qualified adult or permitted capacity.

Demonstrate: Use a verified rule excerpt for a specified license category, then calculate the result for a fully stated group. Do not use invented limits as state law.

Safety feedback: Promising admission before checking an applicable legal capacity limit.

仅凭“多一个孩子”无法判断是否允许。先查实际年龄组合、出勤、合格人员和许可条件，再按现行规则判断。空椅子不等于有合法名额。

Sources still to verify: STATE_CA, STATE_TX, STATE_WA

7. A request for a different tool

Original FAM-058 · new provisional example

Situation

A child has difficulty holding the thin handle used in a painting activity. The family asks whether a larger handle could be used. No individualized review has occurred.

Practice

What should the educator do next?

  1. Reject the request because every child must use identical tools.
  2. Exclude the child from painting.
  3. Tell the family that every request is automatically required, regardless of the facts.
  4. Discuss the child's access needs and consider a workable change that lets the child take part.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: D

Individual review is more useful than a blanket refusal or an automatic promise. Exact legal duties depend on the covered program and applicable law.

Everyday example: A larger handle may let the child explore the same colors and make choices alongside peers.

Demonstrate: Name the learning goal and propose two accessible ways to take part. Review legal applicability separately.

Safety feedback: A blanket disability-based refusal without an individualized review.

先了解儿童的实际需要，再考虑可行调整，例如更粗的画笔手柄。目标是让儿童参与同样有意义的学习，不是一概拒绝，也不是未经分析承诺所有要求都必须满足。

Sources still to verify: ADA_CHILDCARE, NAEYC_DAP, IDEA

8. Payment for an absence

Original FAM-087 · new provisional example

Situation

A provider in another program says every absence is payable. Your own program's current payment terms have not been checked.

Practice

What information should guide your claim?

  1. Your program's current authorization, effective payment terms, and applicable absence rules.
  2. The other provider's experience alone.
  3. A guessed attendance entry that matches the payment requested.
  4. A rule that no absence can ever be paid in any program.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: A

Payment rules depend on the program. An absence does not by itself establish whether payment is permitted, and records must remain accurate.

Everyday example: Two families can use different benefit programs with different payment arrangements even at the same child-care site.

Demonstrate: Using fictional records and verified program instructions, distinguish attendance facts from payment eligibility and explain a correction route.

Safety feedback: Inventing attendance to support a payment claim.

按本项目现行授权、付款条款和缺勤规则判断，不能照搬其他项目经验。出勤事实和可付款条件是不同问题，不能为了账单改造出勤记录。

Sources still to verify: CCDF

9. From toileting help to lunch

Original FAM-034 · new provisional example

Situation

A caregiver finishes helping a child use the toilet and is about to prepare food. Safe supervision is in place while the caregiver completes hygiene steps.

Practice

What should happen before food preparation?

  1. Skip hand hygiene if gloves were worn.
  2. Complete the required handwashing steps and use clean food-handling equipment.
  3. Wipe hands on clothing.
  4. Only check whether the hands look dirty.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: B

Gloves and visible cleanliness do not replace proper hand hygiene. The final lesson should show the current handwashing procedure and applicable facility rules.

Everyday example: A hand can look clean after touching a bathroom door and still need washing before making a sandwich.

Demonstrate: Demonstrate the verified handwashing sequence at a training sink and explain how group supervision is maintained.

Safety feedback: Skipping required hand hygiene before food handling.

如厕协助后，先按正确步骤洗手，再处理食物。戴过手套、手看起来干净，都不能代替手部卫生。最终教材应配上经核实的操作步骤。

Sources still to verify: CDC_HANDS, CDC_DIAPER

10. Describe what happened

Original FAM-056 · new provisional example

Situation

A worker writes, “Ava was bad again.” The worker saw Ava push a block toward Sam after Sam took a truck.

Practice

Which note is most useful for planning support?

  1. Ava is a difficult child.
  2. Ava always behaves badly.
  3. Ava pushed a block toward Sam after Sam took the truck; the adult moved closer and helped them take turns.
  4. Delete the event so the family will not worry.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: C

A concrete observation shows the event, context, and adult response without turning a behavior into a label about the child.

Everyday example: “Spilled the water while reaching for the cup” tells you more than “careless.”

Demonstrate: Rewrite two fictional judgmental notes as observable events. Add one developmentally appropriate support to try and observe.

Safety feedback: No automatic safety-critical failure for wording alone; give specific feedback.

用可观察事实代替“坏”“难管”等标签：谁做了什么、之前发生了什么、成人怎样回应。记录的目的是帮助理解和支持儿童，不是给儿童定性。

Sources still to verify: NAEYC_DAP, CDC_MILESTONES

11. The vehicle is quiet

Original FAM-066 · new provisional example

Situation

Children have left a program vehicle at the destination. The assigned physical vehicle check has not been completed. No child is known to be missing in this example.

Practice

What should the responsible adult do?

  1. Assume the vehicle is empty because it is quiet.
  2. Sign the checklist from memory tomorrow.
  3. Ask a child whether anyone stayed behind and stop there.
  4. Complete the physical vehicle check and the transport plan's other verification steps, while maintaining supervision of the children outside.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: D

Silence is not a reliable check for a child left in a vehicle. A physical check and the applicable safeguards address a risk that a head count alone may miss.

Everyday example: A sleeping child may not answer when someone calls into the vehicle.

Demonstrate: Using an empty parked training vehicle, demonstrate the approved check. A missing child or medical emergency requires the emergency procedure immediately.

Safety feedback: Declaring the vehicle empty without completing the assigned check.

车辆安静不能证明车内没有儿童。按运输计划逐处实查，并完成所要求的其他核实步骤；车外儿童也必须有人照看。若儿童失踪或有紧急危险，应立即启动相应应急流程。

Sources still to verify: NHTSA, HEADSTART

12. Respond without shaming

Original FAM-053 · new provisional example

Situation

During block play, an adult proposes making a child stand in front of the group while everyone says the child is naughty.

Practice

What is the best response?

  1. Stop the humiliating approach and guide the child toward a safe action with calm adult support.
  2. Use the shaming activity if it works quickly.
  3. Ask the other children to decide the punishment.
  4. Ignore any risk because guidance must never include adult limits.
Show answer and feedback

Answer: A

Children need safe limits and support. Public shaming does not teach the next safe action and can harm the child's dignity.

Everyday example: Say, “Blocks stay on the floor. I can help you build here,” while moving close enough to support safe play.

Demonstrate: Practice one brief, respectful limit and one supported alternative suited to the child's age and communication needs.

Safety feedback: Endorsing humiliation as discipline.

停止羞辱做法，用平静、具体的话说明安全界限，并帮助儿童练习下一步。例如：“积木留在地上，我陪你在这里搭。”尊重儿童不等于放弃安全界限。

Sources still to verify: NAEYC_DAP, HEADSTART
