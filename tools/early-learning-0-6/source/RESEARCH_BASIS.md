# Curriculum choice and research status

The user asked for an original U.S. childcare-center curriculum from birth through age six, with the organizing approach chosen after searching popular curricula.

## What was attempted

A popularity search and nine government, professional, publisher and health-reference requests were attempted over HTTPS. Every request failed at the environment proxy with a CONNECT 403 error. An escalated read-only request to the public Head Start government site also failed. No source body was returned or read.

Exact titles, organizations, routes, purposes, timestamps and errors are in `RESEARCH_ATTEMPTS.json`. These are attempted reference routes, not successfully consulted sources. No current national market-share ranking, publisher claim or primary-source quotation is represented as verified.

## The practical decision

Choose an **original play-based, whole-child organization**, with responsive care at its foundation and intentional teaching/inquiry when useful. This gives the center a coherent birth-through-six structure while leaving room for family knowledge, children’s choices, home languages and individual access.

The Creative Curriculum, HighScope and Frog Street are recognizable U.S. early-childhood curriculum families and useful comparison candidates. Their broad descriptions here come from general established knowledge, not a live review in this session. No numerical popularity ranking or claim that this original draft duplicates their coverage, evidence or licensing is made.

Montessori, Reggio-inspired inquiry and Waldorf are educational approaches rather than directly comparable measures of commercial curriculum usage. A center’s public/private/faith-based/Head Start funding or ownership also does not establish its teaching approach. Do not mix these categories into a supposed popularity league table.

## What the resource does

- Seven pathways: birth–5 months, 6–11 months, 12–23 months, 24–35 months, 3 years, 4 years, and 5- and 6-year-olds through 83 months.
- Seven learning areas with approaches-to-learning observations across them.
- Ninety-eight original activity invitations, two in each area/pathway combination.
- A 48-slot inquiry map with four additional calendar weeks left flexible. Babies do not receive a weekly academic theme timetable.
- Planning, factual observation, family input, adaptation and practical follow-up tools.

The Head Start ELOF domain connection is an illustrative author-created organizing map, not a verified current-edition or code-by-code alignment. The framework is a birth-to-five reference; the 5-/6-year-old bridge is original and needs local kindergarten/school coordination. No official assessment, program endorsement, licensing approval or validated developmental norm is created.

## What remains to verify

1. If a popularity ranking is important, obtain a current independently documented representative dataset. Define the year, metric (centers, enrollment, classrooms or teachers), population, program types and treatment of programs using multiple curricula. Publisher reach claims are not independent market-share comparisons.
2. Read current NAEYC DAP and Head Start ELOF/consumer reports. Check the broad organizational crosswalk against the actual texts.
3. Review current program specifications and evidence for any commercial curriculum considered for purchase; this original curriculum does not provide their licensed materials or training.
4. Review applicable state early-learning and kindergarten standards, current health/safety guidance and individualized plans.
5. Have qualified educators and relevant specialists review activity access, infant care, language/literacy, safety, cultural accuracy and local usability. Pilot a manageable subset and hear family feedback.
